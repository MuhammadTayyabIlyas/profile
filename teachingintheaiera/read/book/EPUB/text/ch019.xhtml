Appendix B. Framework map

This map is a reading aid. It shows which official competences each chapter speaks to, so you can follow a thread from the book into a framework. Figure B.1 shows the three frameworks side by side, and the table places each chapter in them. This is an editorial reading, not an equivalence, a qualification or a measure of anyone’s competence. Reading a chapter does not place a teacher at any level.

Three frameworks appear. The third is my own Infrastructure-First Digital Competence framework (IFDC), with four levels: structural provisions, psychological safety, developmental opportunities and strategic practice (Ilyas et al., 2026b). DigCompEdu sets out 22 competences in six areas, with its overview on printed pages 24 and 25 (Redecker, 2017). Its author presents it as support for professional development, not a normative framework or a tool for performance appraisal (p. 28). UNESCO’s AI competency framework for teachers crosses five aspects with three progression levels, Acquire, Deepen and Create (Miao & Cukurova, 2024, p. 22). That grid gives fifteen competency blocks. UNESCO treats competence development as neither hierarchical nor linear, and its levels as a reference pathway (pp. 21 to 22).

Each row names the DigCompEdu competences, the UNESCO aspect and level, and the IFDC level that the chapter’s own Framework connections table gives. Where a placing is my reading rather than the framework’s own words, the note under that chapter’s table says so.

Three rows need a word of caution. Chapter 11 takes its lenses from implementation research, so its DigCompEdu and UNESCO links here are indirect and editorial. For Chapter 12, the higher education chapter, the links describe university assessment. Chapter 13 draws on UNESCO’s human-centred principle, which keeps teachers accountable for pedagogical decisions (Miao & Cukurova, 2024, p. 17). Placing that chapter in the Human accountability block is my interpretation.

Some labels need a note on wording. The map follows the publication’s name for UNESCO’s fifth aspect, AI for professional development (p. 22). The PDF’s short summary says “AI for professional learning” instead. Table 1 spells “organizational” the American way, and the map keeps it. That table joins “AI” and “pedagogy” with a longer dash, where this map uses a plain hyphen.

DigCompEdu here is the published 2017 framework. On 6 November 2026, the Joint Research Centre’s page gave spring 2027 as the date for its updated framework (European Commission, Joint Research Centre, n.d.). Check that page again before you rely on the 2017 labels.
