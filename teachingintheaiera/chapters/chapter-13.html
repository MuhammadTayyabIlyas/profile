Chapter 13. What Endures

From Teaching in the AI Era by Dr Muhammad Tayyab Ilyas

Katie Sanchez’s first instinct was to hand her students a chatbot. She teaches third grade in Sacramento, California, and she had joined a professional learning group on AI. A chatbot could give her young writers feedback on their work. Then she stopped. She liked reading their writing herself. ‘I like to sit down and confer with them,’ she said (Waite et al., 2025, p. 1). So she kept that ‘human connection’ for herself, and gave the machine part of her lesson planning instead.

She did not refuse the tool. She did not hand it to her students either. She decided which part of her work was hers to keep, and why. Every chapter of this book has been leading to that kind of decision.

Questions for reflection

Take these questions to your next department or phase meeting, or into a quiet moment after school.

  1. Which part of your work, like Katie Sanchez’s writing conferences, would you keep for yourself, and how would you explain why?
  2. The last time you used an AI output with students, could you say what you changed and why?
  3. Who in your school can pause or stop a tool, and how would a teacher raise a concern?
  4. What time, data or design work are you absorbing that your school or system should carry?
