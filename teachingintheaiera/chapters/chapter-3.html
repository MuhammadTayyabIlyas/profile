Chapter 3. The Part of Teaching No Machine Can Do

From Teaching in the AI Era by Dr Muhammad Tayyab Ilyas

The oldest teaching aid anyone described to me was not a screen. It was a piece of broken pottery, thousands of years old.

‘Which tools do you mostly use?’ I asked a historian during my doctoral interviews.

‘If we talk about digital tools, I use them, but not extensively,’ he said. In his subjects, ‘tangible objects hold more significance’. So he brings real finds into the room: stones, shards of pottery and other artefacts collected during site exploration at places like Mohenjo-daro and Harappa. They are, in his words, ‘actual items from ancient civilizations’. He opens his laptop only when the real thing is not available (Ilyas, 2026, Participant H02).

Questions for reflection

Take these questions to your next department meeting, or into a quiet moment after school.

  1. In your own class, who would be the first to feel the difference if a machine, and not you, read their work?
  2. When did you last change a lesson halfway through, and what did you notice that made you change it?
  3. How would you know if a student had started turning to a chatbot instead of a person?
  4. Which AI suggestions do you accept without checking, and which do you judge for yourself first?
  5. If AI saved you an hour this week, where would you want that hour to go?
